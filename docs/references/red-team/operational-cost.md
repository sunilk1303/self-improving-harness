# Red-Team Report: Operations & economics pre-mortem

> An engineering-director pre-mortem: eval cost stacks, human attention economics, artifact bloat, vendor churn, on-call burden, scope collapse.
>
> Produced by an adversarial critique agent attacking the winning (GateKit + grafts) design.
> 9 attacks. Severity is 1-5; every attack includes the strongest realistic
> mitigation and an honest residual risk.

## Verdict

Needs changes — the core is salvageable and genuinely better than the field (manifest-as-lockfile deploys, control-domain isolation of evals/policy/audit, typed detectors, durable HITL queues, and rollback-as-rehearsed-invariant are the right bones), but as merged the design is economically unpriced and organizationally overweight. Its costs are concrete and front-loaded (a uniform full-cascade gate at ~$1k-4k per accepted change, 1.5-2 FTE of permanent reviewer/labeler/calibration attention, a nine-component pager surface, and a learned layer whose half-life is one vendor deprecation cycle) while its benefits are soft and back-loaded (synonyms, few-shots, and playbook deltas of undenominated value), and the 20+ judge-panel grafts guarantee phase-1 scope collapse unless frozen. The mostly-mechanical fixes are: (1) tier-proportional gating so Tier 0 costs cents not hundreds of dollars; (2) a hard graft freeze with phase gates and a week-2 user-visible agent demo; (3) human-hours and dollars per accepted improvement as first-class KPIs with a pre-committed downscope criterion; (4) model migration designed as a lifecycle event with the improvement portfolio deliberately weighted toward model-agnostic artifacts (verified SQL, metric definitions, tripwires) over prompt tunings; (5) regression-budget charging gated on causal attribution plus rate-limited ladder dynamics; and (6) proposal admission rationed to measured labeling throughput. Fix those six and this ships; skip them and the most likely death is not a safety incident but a month-6 budget review, an exhausted two-person on-call rotation, and a steering committee that cannot name one thing the loop shipped that an analyst couldn't have config-changed faster.

## Attacks

### [sev 5/5 · high likelihood] The loop consumes the team: standing human-attention obligations sum to 1.5-2+ senior FTEs forever, exceeding the analyst time the agent saves

**Scenario.** Add up the design's permanent human duties: Tier 2 approvals on a 72h SLA, Tier 3 dual approvals, weekly audit of 5 accepted changes, weekly Tier 1 digests, quarterly judge kappa recalibration (200 fresh human labels), the quarterly envelope re-baselining ritual, tripwire and fault-injection authoring, golden-set curation from every production failure, private-slice labeling to feed leakage-budget rotation, ambiguity-tax clarification tickets, and probe-quality reviews for the trust ladder. At even modest proposal volume this is 1.5-2 FTE of exactly the senior data-analyst and platform people the agent was supposed to free up. At month 9 the steering committee asks the killer question: 'name one thing the self-improvement loop shipped that an analyst couldn't have config-changed faster' — and the honest answer is a list of synonyms and few-shots. The team is now staff for the machine, the machine's output is marginal, and the anti-rubber-stamping alarms are (as the design itself predicts) politically resented and disabled first.

**Mitigation.** Budget human-hours-per-accepted-improvement as a dashboarded, capacity-derived metric BEFORE build approval, with named headcount line items in the charter. Aggressively deploy the approval-futures graft so per-item review converts to periodic portfolio review as soon as a change type has survival data. Harvest labels from normal analyst workflow (every resolved ticket auto-drafts a golden candidate) instead of running labeling as a separate ceremony. Pre-commit a kill/downscope criterion: if human-hours-per-improvement or cost-per-improvement exceeds threshold X for two consecutive quarters, the loop freezes to Tier 0 and the roadmap is re-scoped — an exit ramp decided while everyone is still calm.

**Residual risk.** The genuinely uncomfortable possibility that for this workload the optimal 'loop' is a nightly batch of verified-query suggestions reviewed by one analyst — and the architecture has no cheap experiment that discovers this before the full build is sunk cost. Also, kill criteria agreed at kickoff are routinely renegotiated by whoever owns the project when the threshold trips.

### [sev 4/5 · high likelihood] Eval-gate cost stack dwarfs the value of the changes it gates — cost-per-accepted-improvement becomes indefensible at budget review

**Scenario.** A Tier 0 change (add one glossary synonym) runs the same cascade shape as everything else: S2 sandbox replays, S3 = 300 golden questions with 'multiple generations per item' for the paired bootstrap (~900+ agent runs plus judge calls on disagreements, plus multi-perturbed-instance SQL execution burning Snowflake credits), S4 private slice, then >=200 shadow queries at 2x inference with a paired different-family judge, then the counterfactual replay dividend re-runs N days of production traffic through both manifests. At $0.05-0.15 per full agent trajectory, one candidate costs $100-400 in eval alone; with the design's own predicted ~90% rejection rate, cost per ACCEPTED change lands at $1k-4k. Nightly batches of 5-10 proposals put loop spend at $20-60k/month — to ship glossary synonyms, few-shot examples, and playbook line-items whose business value nobody can denominate. At the month-6 spend review, the CFO sees an eval bill larger than the analyst headcount the agent was meant to leverage, and the loop is frozen by finance rather than by any technical failure.

**Mitigation.** Make gating cost proportional to tier and blast radius: Tier 0 gets S1 + S2 + a 30-question smoke slice only, with the full cascade reserved for Tier 1+. Batch compatible Tier 0 deltas into one manifest bump evaluated once per night. Cache eval generations keyed on (manifest_hash, question_id) so unchanged modules don't re-run. Route goldens against local DuckDB copies of the lakeFS snapshots instead of live warehouse credits. Publish cost-per-accepted-improvement and monthly loop spend as first-class dashboard KPIs with a hard monthly cap, reviewed next to a (even if crude) value estimate per change type.

**Residual risk.** Batching destroys per-change attribution (when a batch regresses you must bisect, spending the savings back). DuckDB/warehouse dialect divergence creates false greens. And the value numerator stays soft forever — even a well-run loop remains budget-vulnerable because its costs are precise and its benefits are estimates.

### [sev 4/5 · high likelihood] Accumulated learned artifacts become the new legacy codebase: monotonic growth of VQR entries, playbook deltas, few-shots, and skills degrades quality, latency, and comprehensibility even though every individual item passed its gate

**Scenario.** Month 18: 1,400 verified queries, 600 playbook delta items (dedup-merged but never consolidated), 200 retrieval few-shots, 60 minted skills. Per-request assembled context has crept from 8k to 45k tokens — a 4x silent per-call cost and latency increase that no single gate caught because each delta was individually tiny and the gate compares against 'current baseline', which itself was already bloated. Playbook items from month 3 contradict items from month 14; retrieval sometimes surfaces the stale one. Then the semantic layer is refactored and 400 VQR entries encode the dead schema — the half-life mechanism demotes them to 'stale' but never deletes, so the retrieval index keeps growing and stale-but-still-retrieved bugs appear. No engineer can answer 'why did the router do that' without archaeology across 800 ImprovementRecords. The system is correct-by-construction and incomprehensible-by-accumulation.

**Mitigation.** Impose a global artifact budget enforced at the gate: beyond the cap, adding an item requires evicting one, so a new delta must beat its replacement, not just the baseline. Put per-request assembled-token-count and p95 latency in the S3 guardrail metric set so bloat is charged at admission time. Schedule consolidation (cluster-and-compress the playbook, retire superseded VQR entries) as a recurring first-class Tier 2 change validated by counterfactual replay over real traffic. Make half-life expiry actually tombstone items out of the retrieval index, not just tag them.

**Residual risk.** Consolidation is a mass change — precisely the shape the per-change gate machinery evaluates worst — and with the design's own cited ~34% residual hacking/error floor, some consolidations will silently lose load-bearing knowledge. The comprehensibility problem is only slowed, not solved; the complexity-tax probe prices diffs, not the emergent aggregate.

### [sev 4/5 · high likelihood] Vendor model-upgrade churn invalidates the learned layer wholesale: the half-life of prompt-level improvements is roughly one model deprecation cycle

**Scenario.** Fourteen months in, the vendor announces the pinned generator model sunsets in six months (this happens near-annually), and separately the pinned different-family judge is deprecated with 90 days notice. Every GEPA-evolved prompt, router weight, and few-shot set was tuned against the old generator; the judge's quarterly kappa calibration history is void. The Tier 3 approval for 'model version change' takes ten minutes — the real bill is: re-run GEPA per module, re-validate NL-to-SQL behavior across 1,400 VQR mappings, re-label 200 examples to recalibrate a new judge, and reset the trust ladder because 90-day survival statistics describe the old model's failure modes, not the new one's. That is a full quarter of the team on migration, during which the improvement-velocity SLO craters and — per the ladder's own mechanics — gates re-tighten, compounding the slowdown. The loop's accumulated 'compound interest' turns out to be denominated in a currency the vendor devalues annually.

**Mitigation.** Treat model migration as a designed lifecycle event, not an incident: keep a continuously-evaluated 'next-model' staging manifest (run the public golden set against candidate successor models monthly, so migration day starts from a known gap list). Deliberately weight the improvement portfolio toward model-agnostic artifacts — verified SQL, semantic-layer metric definitions, tripwires, and detector logic survive upgrades; GEPA prompt tunings do not — and let the CMP-lite scheduler discount tuning-type fixes by their expected model-lifetime. Use cloud-platform long-term version pinning (Bedrock/Vertex) where contractually available, and pin the judge on the longest-support track even at capability cost.

**Residual risk.** A frozen judge is fundamentally incompatible with vendor deprecation cadence — some judge discontinuity is unavoidable, and cross-judge score comparability breaks at each swap. Accept and budget that a fixed fraction (plausibly 30-50%) of prompt-layer gains evaporates per generator upgrade; if the ROI case only closes when gains compound indefinitely, it doesn't close.

### [sev 4/5 · high likelihood] On-call and platform-ops burden: nine components across three trust domains, three custom services, and a deploy-blocking drill create a pager surface a platform team would own — assigned to a feature team

**Scenario.** The steady-state stack is FastAPI/DSPy serving, Temporal, OPA sidecar, self-hosted Langfuse, ClickHouse, Grafana, lakeFS, a gVisor sandbox fleet, Postgres audit chain with hourly S3 WORM anchoring, plus three fully custom services (Gatehouse, Limitation Ledger, Deployment Controller). Concrete failure week: the 3am rollback drill starts failing due to a lakeFS branch garbage-collection race — all promotions are blocked for three days while someone debugs a data-versioning system nobody on the team has run in production. The same week, canary auto-rollback flaps twice at 2am on p95 latency during a scheduled warehouse maintenance window, paging the two engineers who also own the improvement backlog. By quarter's end the drill is moved to weekly 'temporarily' and the flappiest rollback trigger is muted — the rollback invariant and the guardrail sensitivity silently rot, which is precisely the Replit-class failure the architecture was built to prevent, now caused by the architecture's own weight.

**Mitigation.** Collapse and buy: managed Temporal Cloud, managed ClickHouse, and the smallest honest substitute for lakeFS at MVP (immutable snapshot naming convention + object versioning) until knowledge-branching is actually needed; ship Gatehouse as a single binary with SQLite before it earns a service. Run the drill in CI on the promotion path, never on the human on-call path, and dampen rollback triggers with mSPRT minimum-effect thresholds plus maintenance-window suppression. Most importantly, put 1.5-2 dedicated platform-ops FTE in the project charter as a precondition — if the org won't fund the pager, descope the architecture now rather than discover it at 2am.

**Residual risk.** The control-domain isolation (separate repos, credentials, processes) that makes the security story credible is irreducible operational complexity — you cannot manage it away, only staff it. And managed-service substitutions collide with the data-residency requirements that motivated self-hosting Langfuse in the first place; some enterprises will force the expensive self-hosted path.

### [sev 4/5 · high likelihood] Graft-accretion scope collapse: the merged design carries 20+ bolted-on mechanisms (Red Twin, Brier ledger, counterfactual replay, Examiner, fault injection, complexity tax, ambiguity tax, futures, half-lives, ...) and the 12-week MVP dies of governance-first sequencing

**Scenario.** The judge panel's grafts each look individually cheap, so the team pulls 'just the easy ones' into phase 1. Week 8 finds two engineers deep in lakeFS copy-on-write knowledge-branch plumbing and hash-chain anchoring while the Router and NL-to-SQL leg — the thing users actually see — is still stubbed. The first demo to leadership is a Grafana dashboard of an audit chain recording zero improvements to an agent that can't yet answer 'what was Q2 revenue by region.' Sponsorship evaporates; the project is cancelled at month 6 with the epitaph 'over-engineered safety apparatus, no product' — the mirror-image failure of the ungoverned agent it was designed to prevent.

**Mitigation.** Enforce a hard graft freeze: every graft gets a phase number, an owner, and an entry criterion tied to evidence (e.g., Red Twin enters only after tripwire pass-rate data exists; Brier ledger only after outcome plumbing exists); none enter phase 1. Require the phase-zero synthetic spike to produce a user-visible working agent by week 2 — demo cadence is the scope regulator. A director-level scope owner with authority to say no to judge-panel enthusiasm is a named role, not a hope.

**Residual risk.** Political: the stakeholders who demanded the grafts sit on the review board and will keep pulling them forward; scope defense is a recurring fight, not a decision. Some grafts (fault injection, self-report diffing) genuinely are cheap and load-bearing, so the freeze needs judgment, which reintroduces negotiation.

### [sev 4/5 · medium likelihood] Labeled-data economics: the design's demand for fresh verified-answer questions grows with proposal volume while supply is a fixed, expensive human process — the leakage budget converts this mismatch into a hard loop stall or gate-discipline decay

**Scenario.** The metered leakage budget means every proposal's private-slice query burns information; at nightly proposal volume the ~50-question private slice exhausts and must rotate roughly monthly. A verified answer to an enterprise BI question needs a senior analyst and a data engineer to agree — 1-2 hours each — so rotation alone is a standing ~100 hour/month labeling operation, on top of judge-calibration labels, golden-set growth, and second-order-reserve maintenance. When labeling lags (it will — it's nobody's favorite job), the org faces the design's own trap: halt private gating and stall the loop, or quietly relax the threshold — and the design itself cites that a +0.01 over-promotion slip destroys the gains. Meanwhile the synthetic reseed graft covers the mechanics but drifts from real analyst question distribution, so over-reliance makes the gates precise about a business that doesn't exist.

**Mitigation.** Adopt the dual-slice split as grafted — synthetic ground-truth-by-construction as the infinite memorization-proof slice, with the small real-traffic slice reserved purely for distribution-fidelity checks — and ration proposal admission rate to measured labeling throughput (the Ledger's bandit should simply not schedule more private-gated proposals than the label supply supports). Instrument label harvesting into the normal analyst workflow so every resolved question/correction auto-drafts a golden candidate with evidence attached.

**Residual risk.** Labeling throughput becomes the binding constraint on improvement velocity, which means the loop's honest steady-state pace is far below its pitch — and the velocity SLO must be set from labeling capacity, or the two-sided error budget will read the labeling bottleneck as 'frozen loop' and agitate to widen gates for the wrong reason.

### [sev 3/5 · high likelihood] Shadow/canary throughput bottleneck: per-candidate shadow of >=200 live queries serializes deployments against real traffic volume, forcing a choice between weeks-long deploy queues, confounded overlapping shadows, or attribution-destroying batches

**Scenario.** The insights agent serves ~300 queries/day in year one (realistic for a single-department rollout). One candidate needs >=200 unserved shadow queries plus a 10% canary soak — roughly a day of exclusive traffic each. The loop approves 5-8 changes a week. Either candidates queue for two-plus weeks (staleness alarms fire, the velocity SLO degrades, and half the queue is invalidated when the manifest under them moves), or the team runs concurrent shadows whose paired-judge comparisons confound each other, or it batches manifests and loses the one-change-one-record audit property that the entire ImprovementRecord schema and CMP-lite credit assignment depend on. Six months in, 'shadow' is quietly redefined as 50 queries to keep the pipe moving, and the anytime-valid statistics no longer have the power to detect the 2-point slice regressions the gate promises to catch.

**Mitigation.** Use logged-traffic replay (previous 7 days through both manifests, paired) as the default 'shadow' for Tier 0/1 — it is parallelizable, off-peak, and statistically identical for changes without live-state dependence — reserving true live shadow for Tier 2/3 and anything touching retrieval freshness. Batch low-tier changes into train-style release manifests on a fixed cadence with automated bisection-on-regression, and record the train membership in each ImprovementRecord so audit granularity survives.

**Residual risk.** Replay misses same-day distribution shifts and live permission/state effects — the exact gap the sandbox-to-prod fidelity risk already names, now doubled down on. Bisection-on-regression works but each bisection burns days and eval dollars, so a bad train is expensive; and train releases mean a known-good fix can wait a week behind the cadence.

### [sev 3/5 · medium likelihood] The trust-ladder governance loop oscillates: noisy regression detectors plus budget-triggered mass tier moves create a control system with delays that nobody tuned, producing flapping between frozen and over-open states

**Scenario.** An upstream team runs a warehouse schema migration; a burst of user-visible wrong answers follows. The regression budget — charged without causal attribution — is spent in a week, auto-demoting Tier 1 change types to Tier 2. The approval queue floods; 72h SLAs blow; expiries auto-reject a month of legitimate proposals; the improvement-velocity SLO fires and auto-generates promotion proposals; the now-swamped reviewers approve them in batches at median 8 seconds each; the rubber-stamping alarm fires and, exactly as the design's own risk register predicts, a VP asks for the alarm to be 'recalibrated.' The ladder has become a driver of instability rather than a governor, and its audit trail now documents the org discrediting its own safety metrics.

**Mitigation.** Charge the regression budget only for regressions causally attributed to a loop-shipped change (counterfactual replay of old-vs-new manifest on the failing queries is the attribution tool the design already has); ambient drift opens limitation events, not budget spend. Rate-limit ladder dynamics: at most one tier transition per change type per quarter, demotions requiring the same evidence standard as promotions, and a queue-load circuit breaker that pauses proposal generation (not approval standards) when reviewer backlog exceeds capacity.

**Residual risk.** Causal attribution in a system of many interacting learned artifacts is genuinely hard; misattribution in both directions persists, and a rate-limited ladder responds sluggishly to real regression clusters — you trade oscillation for lag, and the lag window is where a bad change type keeps shipping.
